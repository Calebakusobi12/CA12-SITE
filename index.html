FOOD SAFETY

5 Everyday Habits for Safer Food Preparation

Good food hygiene starts with simple routines that reduce the chance of contamination at home or in a small food business.

Read article

1. Wash your hands. Use soap and clean running water before preparing food, after handling raw meat, and after using the toilet.

2. Keep raw and ready-to-eat foods separate. Use separate boards and utensils where possible, and keep raw meat away from cooked food.

3. Clean equipment and surfaces. Wash and sanitise food-contact surfaces and utensils appropriately between tasks.

4. Cook food thoroughly. Make sure foods are cooked through, especially meat and fillings; do not rely on the outside colour alone.

5. Store food safely. Cover food, refrigerate perishable items promptly, and follow product-specific storage instructions.

These are general food-hygiene tips. Follow applicable food-safety requirements for your location and business.

FOOD HANDLING

How to Store Meat Pies More Safely

Freshly baked meat pies need careful handling after baking, particularly in warm weather.

Read article

After baking, handle meat pies with clean hands or clean utensils and protect them from dust, insects and unnecessary contact.

Do not leave meat-filled pastries at room temperature for extended periods. As a general food-safety rule, refrigerate perishable food within two hours, or within one hour when the surroundings are hotter than 32°C.

Keep refrigerated pies covered and cold. Use them within a few days, and discard food that has been stored unsafely or shows signs of spoilage. Smell and appearance alone cannot guarantee that food is safe.

When reheating, heat the filling thoroughly until hot throughout. For commercial production, establish and follow a documented time-and-temperature control process that meets local requirements.

Storage life depends on ingredients, preparation, packaging and temperature. These general tips are not a substitute for a validated food-safety plan.

FOOD LITERACY

How to Read a Packaged Food Label

Food labels help shoppers understand what a product contains and how it should be stored and used.

Read article

Check the product name and ingredients. Ingredients are generally listed in descending order by weight, so the first ingredients make up the largest proportions.

Look for date information. Understand the meaning of the date mark on the product and follow any storage instructions. Do not ignore signs of damaged packaging.

Check allergens. Review the ingredients and allergen declarations carefully if you or someone you are buying for has a food allergy.

Read nutrition information. Where provided, compare serving sizes and values such as energy, sugar, salt and fat rather than comparing numbers without checking the serving basis.

Follow storage instructions. A product may need refrigeration after opening even if it was shelf-stable before opening.

Label requirements differ by product and jurisdiction. Refer to the relevant regulator for official guidance.
